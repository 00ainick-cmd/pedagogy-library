# Job-to-Card Chooser

Use this table to pick the right card type before you open the builder.
Cross-reference the swap rules in [01-swap-protocol-checklist.md](01-swap-protocol-checklist.md)
and the full sequence recipes in [03-pedagogy-recipes.md](03-pedagogy-recipes.md).

---

## How to choose (3 steps)

1. **Name the job.** Ask: what must the learner DO in this beat? Not "what topic does it cover"
   but what cognitive act it demands (open, predict, recall, sequence, discriminate, etc.).

2. **Match the job to the card.** Find that job in the table below. Take the card it maps to.
   If two cards could work, read the "use when" column for the tiebreaker.

3. **Swap the content, keep the pattern.** Copy the card's interaction shape and AERO register.
   Replace only the items marked Always in [01-swap-protocol-checklist.md](01-swap-protocol-checklist.md).
   Never redesign the card for a new topic.

## Decision table

| Learning job | Card | # | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open / motivate | Cold Open | 01 | First beat of a module; set the topic with a hook that creates tension, not a greeting. |
| Frame objectives | The Contract | 02 | Immediately after the open; state what the learner will do using performance verbs. |
| Teach a concept | Concept Page | 03 | A textual concept needs a claim, a term lockup, and two or three short distinctions. |
| Prove with one figure | The Number | 04 | One real statistic frames the problem and needs to land big before anything else. |
| Prove a relationship | The Evidence | 05 | A trend, distribution, or comparison that a single number cannot show. |
| Active recall | Reveal Board | 06 | The learner has just been taught a set of distinctions and needs to retrieve them by flipping tiles. |
| Explore a system | Explorable Schematic | 07 | Replace a labeled-diagram lecture; the learner interrogates parts by tapping them. |
| Apply / manipulate an input | Live Instrument | 08 | The learner needs to feel how one input drives a reading and where pass/fail sits. |
| Perform an ordered procedure | Sequence It | 09 | A procedure where order carries a real consequence and must be practiced, not just read. |
| Discriminate good vs. bad | Compare Wipe | 10 | Teach the eye to tell in-tolerance from out, or correct from defective. |
| Predict before proof | Predict, then Reveal | 11 | Force a commitment before showing data so the result lands with weight. |
| Perform a skill through judgment | Branching Scenario | 12 | An ABCD-format objective (Demonstrate a callout, a turnover, a briefing) needs a real decision tree with consequences, not a recognition question. |

## Choosing between near-neighbors

A few jobs sit close together. These distinctions help:

- **03 vs. 06** (teach vs. recall): Use 03 to introduce; use 06 after teaching, to make the learner
  retrieve the distinctions themselves. They often appear in sequence.

- **04 vs. 05** (one number vs. a chart): If the point fits in one figure, use 04. If the point
  requires a comparison across values or time, use 05.

- **07 vs. 08** (explore vs. manipulate): Use 07 when the learning goal is "what does each part
  do." Use 08 when the goal is "what happens when I change this input."

- **09 vs. 11** (sequence vs. predict): Use 09 when step order is the skill being assessed.
  Use 11 when you want the learner to commit to an outcome before seeing real data.

## On the bench (designed, not yet built)

These six ideas are cataloged for future build but have no card number yet:

- **Layer X-ray:** toggle down through a cutaway, peeling layers from outside to backplane.
- **Sort into buckets:** drag or tap items into categories (Dirty Dozen factors, GO/NO-GO, tool classes).
- **Calculation sandbox:** plug numbers into a real formula with a live result and a pass/fail threshold.
- **Scrub the signal:** drag a playhead along a signal-flow path and watch a packet move stage to stage.
- **Spot the defect:** tap the fault in a scene, with hit/miss feedback.
- **Confidence-rated recall:** rate certainty before flipping; the gap between confidence and correctness
  is the lesson.
