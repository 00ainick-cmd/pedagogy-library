Predict, Then Reveal

Before the page shows what happens, the student commits to a prediction. Then the reveal shows what really happens next to the prediction, and explains why. Any choice is accepted: a prediction is not graded.

From the lesson Blocked Ports and Altimeter Setting (Flight Instruments), page 8, "Airspeed". Open it in the real lesson

Lesson page, as the student sees it
Static side

Airspeed with the Static System Blocked

The airspeed indicator compares two pressures: ram pressure from the pitot line, inside its diaphragm, and static pressure from the static line, in its case. When the static line blocks, the case keeps the pressure it had at that moment.

Pitot line

Carries ram pressure, the pressure of the air stopped in the pitot tube. It rises with speed, and it falls as the airplane climbs into thinner air.

Case, static line blocked

Holds the static pressure from the altitude where the line blocked. It does not change when the airplane climbs or descends.

The static line blocks at 8,500 feet while the airplane cruises at 120 knots. It keeps flying at 120 knots. Predict what the airspeed indicator shows at each altitude, then select Check.

Each bar shows a pressure in inches of mercury (in Hg). The needle reads the difference between the pitot line and the case.

Climb

Outside pressure falls, so ram pressure falls with it, while the case holds the higher trapped pressure. The difference is too small, and the airspeed reads low.

Descent

Outside pressure rises, so ram pressure rises, while the case holds the lower trapped pressure. The difference is too large, and the airspeed reads high.

The airspeed reads right only at the blockage altitude. The farther the airplane goes from it, up or down, the larger the error. At this speed, 500 feet is enough for an error of 30 to 45 knots.

Why It Teaches

Committing to a prediction, even a wrong one, improves memory for the answer that follows. A 2024 meta-analysis of prequestions found an effect of about g = 0.54 on the predicted items (St. Hilaire, Chan and Ahn). The prediction error is what does the work.

So the options are the three real possibilities, the reveal comes on the same page within seconds, and the student's own pick stays marked beside what happens. The reveal is corrective feedback on the student's model of the instrument, not a score.

It needs a basis for the guess. In the lesson, this page comes after the pages that teach the pitot line, the static line and what a blockage traps, which is why the two cards above restate them.

How It Is Built

The numbers are not typed in. The page computes them with the lesson's own pressure model: the standard atmosphere gives the outside pressure at each altitude, and the airspeed indicator reads the pitot line minus its case. At 9,000 feet that gives about 76 knots, at 8,000 feet about 152 knots, and the bars fill from those pressures.

Predict, then reveal is a local part. The shared ask card says "Not quite" and asks to try again, which suits a check better than a prediction. The quoted handbook text is the real FAA wording, shown on its own paper.
