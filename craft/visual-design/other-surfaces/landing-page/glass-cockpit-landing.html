The question bank isn't the problem. Your feedback loop is.

Most prep is a thousand questions, an answer key, and a prayer. You grind the whole bank, including the 70% you already know, and walk into the test still guessing about the 30% you don't.

FAULT 01 Rote grinding

You drill what you already know

Re-reading the full bank treats your strong areas like weak ones. That's hours burned on questions you'd never miss, and minutes left for the ones failing you.

FAULT 02 No provenance

Answer keys with no receipts

"The answer is C" teaches you to memorize letters, not systems. Without the source (the handbook, the chapter, the principle), the knowledge evaporates after test day.

FAULT 03 Zero adaptation

The bank doesn't know you

A static PDF can't tell that you're solid on Ohm's Law but shaky on transponders. So it can't fix it. Every session starts from zero, every time.

ACE drills run a different loop: every miss is diagnosed to its ACS knowledge code, cited to the exact FAA handbook it came from, and queued for re-attack until your mastery bar says SOLID.
