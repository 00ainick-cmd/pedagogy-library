# Image Licensing for History and Incident Pages

Nick approved downloads for lesson portraits and photos on the condition that the licence is verified. This is the
checklist that verifies it, the trusted sources, and the credit line format. It is practical guidance from the sources'
own published terms, not legal advice.

## 1. The rule in one paragraph

Use an image only when its own file page says it is in the public domain in the United States or carries a free licence
whose terms the lesson can meet. Read the licence on the file page, not on a search result or a copy elsewhere. Prefer
public domain. For portraits, use public domain only. For photos of aircraft, parts and places, a CC BY or CC BY-SA
licence is acceptable when no public-domain image does the job, with the full credit and licence link on the page. Never
use NC (non-commercial), ND (no derivatives), "used with permission", "all rights reserved", or an image whose source
you cannot trace.

## 2. Checklist (do every step, every image)

1. **Open the file page itself.** For Wikimedia Commons, the page at `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:...`. For
   the Library of Congress, the item page. Save the URL in the page copy file.
2. **Read the licence box.** Note the exact template or statement (for example "PD-US-expired", "PD-USGov-NTSB",
   "No known restrictions on publication", "CC BY-SA 2.0").
3. **Check it is public domain in the United States**, by one of these:
   - published before 1 January 1931 (Commons, US rules: "Anything (other than sound recordings) published before
     January 1, 1931 is in the public domain");
   - a work of the US federal government made by an employee in official duties (17 U.S.C. 105: "Copyright protection
     under this title is not available for any work of the United States Government"); state and local government
     works are not covered;
   - a US work published 1931 to 1963 whose copyright was not renewed, or 1931 to 1977 without a notice (Commons
     templates "PD-US-not renewed", "PD-US-no notice"): acceptable, but the claim rests on Commons volunteers' research,
     so treat it as medium risk and prefer another image when one exists;
   - CC0 or an explicit public domain dedication by the rights holder.
4. **Check the source and the author.** The file page must name a credible source (an archive, a museum, a library, an
   agency, a publication with its date). A file whose source is a private website or "own work" for a historical photo
   is a warning sign. Example found on 2026-10-01: a 1929 photo of Doolittle's blind flight tagged CC BY-SA 4.0 by an
   uploader who took it from a book. That licence cannot be right; do not use it.
5. **Check for a second rights layer.** A photo of a painting is public domain in the US when the painting is (Commons
   "PD-Art"); a museum's own photo of a 3D object (an instrument, a model) can carry the photographer's rights. Logos and
   insignia (NASA, ARINC, airline marks) are trademarks; do not feature them.
6. **Check the date and the death year match the claim.** A photo dated 1933 by a French agency, tagged public domain
   for France, may still be under US copyright. Example: an Agence Rol portrait of d'Arsonval, 1933. Pick a pre-1931
   image instead.
7. **Look at the image.** It must show who or what the caption says, at a usable size. Low-resolution files (under about
   400 px wide) will look poor on a phone hero.
8. **Write the credit line** (section 5) and the alt text, and record the licence in the page copy file and in a
   `CREDITS.md` beside the image in the lesson's assets folder (the AC Principles lesson does this).
9. **Download** the original or a large size from the file page, crop or resize only, and note "cropped" in the credit
   if you cropped.

## 4. When no free image exists

Draw it. The built lessons already do this well: "Drawn from NTSB report AAR-89/03" (the Aloha lap joint), "Drawing made
for this lesson from the NTSB description (Safety Alert SA-054)". A drawing:
- shows only what the report describes, and is labelled as a drawing made for the lesson, with the report cited;
- does not copy a copyrighted figure line for line; redraw from the facts (dimensions, positions, parts named);
- is the right choice for incident pages anyway, because it can show the part and the fault without wreckage.

Never use an AI-generated likeness of a real person, and never present a drawing as a photograph.

## 5. Credit line format

On the page, under the image or on the page's credit line, in this order:

`[Subject], [kind of image] by [author], [date]. [Holding institution]. [Licence], via [where you got it].`

Link the subject's name to the file page. Examples that follow the built lessons:

- Portrait, public domain: "Portrait: Joseph Henry, photograph by Mathew Brady or Levin Handy, 1865 to 1880, Library of
  Congress. Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons."
- Government photo: "Photo: National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB), public domain, via Wikimedia Commons."
- Handbook figure: "Source: FAA-H-8083-30B, Figure 14-19."
- CC BY-SA photo: "Photo: [Author], Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0 (link the licence). Cropped."
- OGL figure: "Source: AAIB report 1/92. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence
  v3.0."

In the lesson's assets folder, add a `CREDITS.md` line for each image: file name, file page URL, author, date, licence
as written on the file page, date checked.

## 6. Quick decision table

| The file page says | Use it? |
|---|---|
| PD-old / PD-US-expired / published before 1931 | Yes |
| PD-USGov (any US federal agency), PD-USGov-NTSB, -FAA, -NASA, -Military | Yes, if the agency employee made it |
| No known restrictions (LOC) and the photo is pre-1931 or US government | Yes |
| PD-US-not renewed / PD-US-no notice | Yes with care; prefer another image if one exists |
| CC0 | Yes |
| CC BY | Yes, with author, licence and link |
| CC BY-SA | Aircraft, part and place photos only, with author, licence and link; note any crop |
| Open Government Licence v3.0 | Yes, with the OGL attribution line |
| Licence Ouverte (France, Etalab) | Yes, with attribution to the source |
| GFDL only | Avoid (the licence text must travel with the image) |
| CC BY-NC, CC BY-ND, "used with permission", "all rights reserved", no licence | No |
| Licence claimed by an uploader who did not make a historical photo | No |

## 7. Known flags carried over from the retired asset manifest

The old Art Library's visual asset manifest (retired 2026-10-01, see `../visual-design/retired-art-library.md`) flagged
two licence problems. They still stand:

- **Do not ship** the two legacy interface extracts from the old ACE Atlas course player (`atlas-interface-extract/flipcheck.png`
  and an auto-named pasted mockup in its `uploads/` folder). Their source and licence are unknown; keep them out of
  every lesson until the licence is cleared.
- **Confirm the source before shipping** the two NTSB report 2574 photos (the stabilizer and the leading edge) in the
  video studio's portfolio assets. NTSB staff photos are US government works and public domain, but a report can
  include third-party photos (section 3, NTSB row); check the specific photo's credit in the report before using it in a
  paid course.
