# Mixing Guide

## The standard

Nick, on the first 19 rebuilt lessons (2026-09-30): "This is by far the best training I've ever seen in my life." And in the same breath: "Keep every lesson fresh, not a template. Do not copy another lesson's page sequence or reuse the same set of interactions. Pick the interactions that fit this topic and mix them up: a lesson that leans on sorts and flip cards next to one that leans on a live instrument, a signal you tune, a scenario you run, a part you take apart in 3D."

Freshness is not decoration. Every interaction on a page must still pass one test: does it make the student think about the thing the page teaches? Behavior is not learning (Mayer 2004), interacting with a model helps only when it gets the student to see what matters (Keehner et al. 2008), and material that is interesting but extra hurts learning (Rey 2012). Variety comes from fitting the interaction to the content, not from rotating a list.

## Four families

Every pattern in this catalog belongs to one family. A good lesson uses all four.

| Family | The student | Patterns |
|---|---|---|
| **Explore** (show) | looks, selects, compares | [hotspot-on-real-document](hotspot-on-real-document.md), [labeled-graphic](labeled-graphic.md), [tabs](tabs.md), [accordion](accordion.md), [flip-cards](flip-cards.md), [process-stepper](process-stepper.md), [timeline](timeline.md), [image-compare-slider](image-compare-slider.md), [state-toggle-figure](state-toggle-figure.md), [3d-object-explorer](3d-object-explorer.md), [interactive-video](interactive-video.md), [case-file](case-file.md) |
| **Check** (retrieve) | answers, sorts, finds, orders | [ask-card](ask-card.md), [picture-option-question](picture-option-question.md), [predict-then-reveal](predict-then-reveal.md), [one-card-sort](one-card-sort.md), [drag-to-order](drag-to-order.md), [matching](matching.md), [find-the-hotspot](find-the-hotspot.md) |
| **Do** (perform) | works the job on a model | [live-model](live-model.md), [guided-procedure](guided-procedure.md), [find-the-fault](find-the-fault.md), [build-the-circuit](build-the-circuit.md), [build-the-record](build-the-record.md), [worked-example](worked-example.md), [fill-the-table](fill-the-table.md), [scenario-branching](scenario-branching.md), [experience-demo](experience-demo.md) |
| **Carry** (keep) | reads a block that holds the key fact, takes it to work | [term-card](term-card.md), [stat-card](stat-card.md), [checklist](checklist.md), plus the shared rule box, pull quote, callout, step cards and the field card |

The "do" family is where Chi and Wylie's ICAP framework places the deepest engagement: constructing something (an entry, a circuit, a diagnosis) beats manipulating something given, which beats watching.

## Content type to interaction

Pick from the content, then check the variety rules below.

| Content | First choices | Also good | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Procedure** (install, remove, test, respond) | [process-stepper](process-stepper.md) to show it, then [drag-to-order](drag-to-order.md) to recall it, then [guided-procedure](guided-procedure.md) to do it | [checklist](checklist.md) as the job aid; [predict-then-reveal](predict-then-reveal.md) on the step people get wrong | Steps hidden in an accordion; flip cards for steps; the stepper and the order on the same page |
| **Regulation, standard, form, data sheet** | [hotspot-on-real-document](hotspot-on-real-document.md) with lookups | [build-the-record](build-the-record.md); [one-card-sort](one-card-sort.md) (meets the rule or not); [scenario-branching](scenario-branching.md) for the judgment call; [stat-card](stat-card.md) for the rule's numbers | A paraphrase in place of the real text; tabs that hide paragraphs of the rule |
| **Concept** (how a thing works) | [labeled-graphic](labeled-graphic.md) first (names the parts), then [state-toggle-figure](state-toggle-figure.md) or [live-model](live-model.md) with a prediction | [3d-object-explorer](3d-object-explorer.md) when the shape teaches; [flip-cards](flip-cards.md) for symbol to part; [picture-option-question](picture-option-question.md) to check | Free play with no task; a stepper or a compare wipe for something that moves (Nick asked for an animated video instead) |

## The variety rules

1. **One idea and one doing moment per page.** Every page has at least one interaction or graphical block, and never two plain paragraph blocks in a row (the brief). Most pages have exactly one interaction.
2. **No two pages in a row with the same main interaction.** Ask cards count as a type. A sort, then a sort, is a quiz page; split them with a figure or a model. A short ask card under a page's main interaction is fine.
3. **At least six different types in a 15 to 21 page lesson, and no type more than three times.** Counting the Recall card, ask cards may appear four times.
4. **Every lesson has four anchors**:
   - one **real thing**: the real regulation, data sheet, FAA figure, form, instrument sim or 3D part;
   - one **do** page: guided procedure, live model with a task, find the fault, or a build;
   - at least one **picture question**, in the lesson or in the check (Nick: "I like images bc it freshens it up");
   - one **signature interaction** that no other lesson in the same room uses the same way (a tuned signal, a breaker panel, a T-tail you turn, a regulation you search).
5. **Never three explore pages in a row.** After two pages of showing, ask the student to retrieve or do something.
6. **Show, check, do.** Inside a topic, show it (labeled graphic, stepper), check it (sort, order, ask), then do it (model, procedure, fault). Put a prediction before the show only when the student has something to base it on.
7. **Do not copy a neighbour's sequence.** Before planning, write down the interaction sequence of the two lessons before this one in the room. Change at least half of the types, and never reuse their order. The brief: "Do not copy another lesson's page sequence or reuse the same set of interactions."
8. **Give big interactions their own page.** A sort, a sim or a reader that does not fit one phone screen with the content above it goes on its own page (safety-data-sheets puts its sorts and its scene on their own pages for this reason).
9. **Content first.** Choose from the content table, then apply rules 2 to 8. Never pick an interaction because the last lesson used it, or because it is new.

## Before you show Nick

- Count the types. At least six, none more than three times, no main interaction on two pages in a row.
- Name the four anchors: the real thing, the do page, the picture question, the signature interaction.
- Compare with the two lessons before it in the room. Different sequence?
- Every interaction passes "what does this make the student think about?"
- No key content in an accordion or behind a click.
- Every interaction works by tap, by keyboard and on a 390 px phone, and nothing moves under reduced motion that does not need to.
- No cute headings, no chips, no lesson codes, no em or en dashes.
