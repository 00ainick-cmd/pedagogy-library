# Competency-Based Training and Assessment (CBTA)

## One-line claim

Define each job competency as observable behaviours performed under stated conditions to a stated standard, train directly to those behaviours, and advance a learner only when multiple observations by calibrated assessors, across varied real or simulated contexts, show integrated performance at the standard, however long that takes.

## Evidence base

CBTA replaces "completed the hours" with "demonstrated the performance." Frank et al. (2010), an international consensus group writing in *Medical Teacher*, defined competency-based medical education as an outcomes approach organized around competencies that promotes learner-centredness and de-emphasizes time-based curricular design, and described competence as multi-dimensional, dynamic, developmental, and contextual; the paper is a definitional consensus, not an outcome trial. Aviation codified the same logic. ICAO's *Procedures for Air Navigation Services: Training* (PANS-TRG, Doc 9868, third edition, 2020) defines a performance criterion as an observable behaviour, its conditions, and a competency standard, and its Part I principles state that assessor judgements are calibrated for inter-rater reliability, that assessment rests on multiple observations across multiple contexts, and that competence means integrated performance of all required competencies to the standard (2.2.8-2.2.10); Part III extends the approach, as an optional programme, to aircraft maintenance personnel. In the United States, the FAA's Part 147 interim final rule (87 FR 31391, May 24, 2022, effective September 21, 2022, adopted as final without change on June 13, 2023) removed the prescriptive curriculum-hour requirements (a 1,900-hour core set in 1970) and the appendix subject lists, required each school to keep a curriculum designed to continually align with FAA-S-ACS-1, *Aviation Mechanic General, Airframe, and Powerplant Airman Certification Standards* (November 1, 2021), and ended FAA curriculum approval; the FAA described the intent as "a more flexible, performance-based standard." The ACS lists coded knowledge, risk management, and skill elements per subject and became the mechanic testing standard after July 31, 2023; the tests still carry a 70 percent minimum passing grade (14 CFR 65.17) and an oral and practical test given by an evaluator.

The best evidence concerns assessment design rather than programme outcomes. Van der Vleuten and Schuwirth (2005) used a utility model in *Medical Education* to argue that reliability, validity, and educational impact are not properties of an instrument but depend on adequate sampling, and that complex competence requires quantitative and qualitative information from different sources plus professional judgement, so the unit of design is the assessment programme, not the single test. Holmboe et al. (2010) specified what that programme must be in competency-based education: continuous and frequent, criterion-based, developmental, work-based where possible, and mixing quantitative and qualitative methods. The sampling claim has numbers behind it: Moonen-van Loon et al. (2013) ran generalizability analyses on 12,779 workplace-based assessments from 953 residents in *Advances in Health Sciences Education* and found that a reliability of 0.80 required eight mini-CEXs, nine DOPS, or nine multisource-feedback rounds used alone, or seven mini-CEXs, eight DOPS, and one multisource-feedback round combined in a portfolio. Ten Cate (2013) turned the aggregate into a decision: an entrustable professional activity is a task entrusted to unsupervised execution once the trainee has attained sufficient specific competence, entrustment requires observed proficiency usually on multiple occasions, and the decision is graded on five supervision levels running from observation only to supervising junior colleagues.

Outcome evidence for competency-based programmes as a whole is thin and mixed, which is why this chapter rates the evidence moderate. In a *JAMA* systematic review, Kogan, Holmboe, and Hauer (2009) found 55 direct-observation tools; only 11 had validity evidence from internal structure and relationship to other variables, and objectively measured change in knowledge or skills was reported for 5. Miller and Archer's (2010) *BMJ* systematic review found 16 studies, 15 of them non-comparative, and no evidence that the mini-CEX, DOPS, or case-based discussion improved performance; multisource feedback changed practice mainly when the feedback was credible or coaching was provided. The defensible claim is therefore narrower than the slogan: observable definitions plus wide sampling make progression decisions more valid and reliable, while the reviews cited here do not show that the assessment tools themselves improve performance. CBTA is distinct from [mastery-learning](../02-instructional-design/mastery-learning.md) (the instructional cycle of formative test, correctives, and parallel retest), from [mastery-threshold-transfer-test-design](../03-assessment-science/mastery-threshold-transfer-test-design.md) (cut score and transfer items on one test), and from [mastery-thresholds](../07-runtime-decisions/mastery-thresholds.md) (the runtime cutoff rule): CBTA defines what competence looks like on the job and how much observed evidence a sign-off needs.

## When to apply

- **Competency model needed.** A role, certificate, or job task is becoming a course. Write the adapted
  competency model first: competency, description, observable behaviours, conditions, standard (ICAO,
  2020, Part I, 2.5), derived from a task analysis, not from a textbook's chapter list.
- **Seat-time completion rule detected.** The course completes on hours logged, slides viewed, or one
  recall quiz. Replace it with an evidence rule; PANS-TRG asks programmes to focus on what trainees do
  and achieve rather than prescribed time (Part I, Attachment A).
- **Practical assessment design started.** A sim, lab practical, oral, or on-the-job check is being
  built. Write the rubric from the indicators, set job-matched conditions, and plan observations first.
- **Performance observation logged.** A sim attempt, lab check, or supervised task enters the portfolio.
  Check coverage (indicators, contexts, assessors), feed back on misses, and vary the next context.
- **Sign-off or entrustment decision pending.** The learner nears a gate. Aggregate the portfolio
  against the rule: enough observations, contexts, and assessors, and no unresolved critical errors
  (ten Cate, 2013; Moonen-van Loon et al., 2013).
- **Regulatory standard alignment review.** A curriculum must show alignment to FAA-S-ACS-1, a
  PANS-TRG framework, or an accreditation standard. Map each element to a competency and its evidence.

## When NOT to apply

- **Target performance cannot yet be specified.** New equipment with no maintenance data, or an
  unanalysed role, gives no basis for behaviours or a standard. Run the task analysis first
  ([cognitive-task-analysis](../02-instructional-design/cognitive-task-analysis.md)).
- **The objective is isolated verbal recall.** A frequency, regulation number, or definition needs
  retrieval practice and an item check ([testing-effect](../01-learning-science/testing-effect.md)),
  not an observation portfolio.
- **Learner is at first exposure.** Early attempts are formative: rate them and feed back, but keep
  them out of the sign-off until the learner has had instruction and practice on the whole task.
- **The calendar cannot let time vary.** If every learner exits on the same date regardless of
  evidence, CBTA becomes time-based training with extra paperwork. Add re-observation slots or label
  the course honestly as time-based.
- **The simulation used as evidence is unverified.** A sim whose values, limits, and faults have not
  been checked against the governing standard and equipment manual cannot supply sign-off evidence
  ([simulation-fidelity](../04-delivery-patterns/simulation-fidelity.md)).

## How to apply

- **Write each indicator as behaviour, condition, standard.** Behaviour: an observable verb
  ("measures," "rejects"), never "understands." Conditions: equipment, references, environment,
  supervision. Standard: the tolerance or decision rule from the governing document, with its source.
- **Assess the whole task; decompose only for teaching.** PANS-TRG warns that learners can perform
  tasks in isolation without being competent in the job (Part I, Attachment A). Teach part-tasks as
  needed; make the sign-off observation the whole task ([4c-id-model](../02-instructional-design/4c-id-model.md)).
- **Train to the indicators the assessment uses.** Practice shares the assessment's conditions and
  rubric, so feedback names the indicator, the observed value, and the standard
  ([deliberate-practice](../01-learning-science/deliberate-practice.md)).
- **Plan the sample, not the test.** Per competency, set the minimum observations, the contexts they
  must span (equipment variants, fault types, environments), and the minimum number of assessors.
  Start from seven to nine observations per tool for 0.80 reliability (Moonen-van Loon et al., 2013).
- **Calibrate assessors on shared exemplars.** All assessors rate the same recorded performances,
  compare indicator by indicator, and resolve disagreements against the standard (ICAO, 2020, 2.2.8).
- **Make safety-critical indicators non-compensatory.** One critical error (passing an out-of-limit
  unit, skipping a required test) fails the observation, as NREMT skill-sheet critical criteria do.
- **Separate low-stakes observations from the high-stakes decision.** Each observation is feedback;
  the gate decision aggregates the portfolio with professional judgement (van der Vleuten &
  Schuwirth, 2005), is recorded with its evidence, and is stated as a supervision level (ten Cate, 2013).

## Common misapplications

- **Relabelling a topic list as competencies.** "Understands transponder theory" names no behaviour,
  condition, or standard and cannot be observed.
- **Checklist atomization.** Forty steps ticked on separate days never show the integrated
  performance PANS-TRG requires (Part I, 2.2.10).
- **One observation, one signature.** One passed practical is one sample; reliable high-stakes
  judgement needs many (van der Vleuten & Schuwirth, 2005; Moonen-van Loon et al., 2013).
- **Competency language, seat-time mechanics.** The LMS still completes on time in module, and every
  learner advances on the same day.
- **One rater, never calibrated.** A single uncalibrated assessor's ratings measure the assessor as
  much as the learner; spread observations across calibrated assessors.
- **Reading the FAA change as "no standard."** Removing hours did not remove the floor: the curriculum
  must align with the ACS, and applicants still face the knowledge test and the oral and practical.
- **Treating a cut score as a competency decision.** An 80 percent quiz is one data source, not
  observed performance; see [mastery-threshold-transfer-test-design](../03-assessment-science/mastery-threshold-transfer-test-design.md).

## Examples across domains

**Avionics (CAET lesson): ATC transponder test as a signed-off competency.**

*Setup.* A CAET transponder module ends with a 10-item quiz at 80 percent and marks complete. The
designer rewrites "perform an ATC transponder test per 14 CFR 91.413 and Part 43 Appendix F" as a
competency. Behaviours: configures the ramp test set per its manual, runs each Appendix F item that
applies (items (e)-(j) are Mode S only), compares each reading to the class-correct limit, runs the
Appendix E(c) altitude check when altitude reporting equipment was installed or maintained
(91.413(b)), decides pass or reject, and writes the record entry. Conditions: aircraft on the ground,
manuals at hand, work under a repair station holding the rating 91.413(c) requires. Standards: reply
frequency 1090 ±3 MHz (ATCRBS) or ±1 MHz (Mode S classes 1A, 2A, 3A, 4); MTL -73 ±4 dBm (ATCRBS) or
-74 ±3 dBm (Mode S); class minimum peak power 21.0 dBW (125 W) or 18.5 dBW (70 W), maximum 27.0 dBW
(500 W); Mode S replies only to its assigned address; Mode C within 125 feet of the altimeter.

*Sim-based practical and rubric.* The learner sees a ramp scene and a simulated test-set panel,
selects test items, logs readings on a test record, and decides. Scenarios span three aircraft whose
transponder classes change the limits, with seeded faults: a Mode S unit replying at 1091.4 MHz
(passes ±3 MHz, fails ±1 MHz), a replaced encoder reporting 200 feet off the altimeter, a unit
answering an incorrect address, and one healthy unit to catch false rejects. Passing an out-of-limit
value, using the wrong class limit, or skipping E(c) after encoder work is a critical error.

*Mastery gate and follow-up.* Sign-off needs four consecutive clean attempts covering all three
aircraft and at least two seeded faults, reviewed by two different instructors. Some learners get
there in two sessions, others in six; the ADS-B Out module unlocks on the evidence, not time in
course. The sim sign-off records supervision level 2 (direct supervision; ten Cate, 2013); the repair
station makes the on-aircraft entrustment later, from observed tests on real aircraft. If most of a
cohort misses the class-limit indicator, the designer adds a worked example before the practical.

**Paramedic and EMS certification: capstone team leads as entrustment evidence.**

*Setup.* An accredited paramedic program once signed students off after a set of ambulance hours.
CoAEMSP's interpretations of the 2023 CAAHEP Standards require it to track minimum competency numbers
based on CoAEMSP's Student Minimum Competency recommendations, including pediatric age subgroups; to
define a successful team lead and set a minimum number; to train capstone preceptors on that
definition; and to have the medical director sign a terminal competency form for each graduate.

*Competency and observations.* The designer writes "leads out-of-hospital assessment and management
of an adult with chest pain." Behaviours: scene safety, primary assessment, 12-lead acquisition and
interpretation, protocol-based treatment, team direction, transport decision, handoff report.
Conditions progress from scenario lab on a manikin, to hospital clinical with a preceptor, to the
capstone field internship as team leader on an ALS unit. Standard: "successful; competent; no
prompting necessary" on the NREMT scale, with critical criteria as automatic failures. Observations
span ages, complaints, and several preceptors; the 2015 NREMT portfolio pilot criteria required 18
of the last 20 capstone patient contacts rated successful in team leadership.

*Decision and follow-up.* The program director and medical director sign terminal competency only
when tracked minimums and the team-lead rule are met; hours remain a program floor, not the gate. A
student who falls short gets more capstone shifts, not a signature for time served, matching the 2015
NREMT definition of entry-level competency as consistent, safe performance over multiple patient
encounters. The advisory committee reviews the minimums, including team leads, every year.

## Quality signal

CBTA is working when (a) every sign-off rests on the planned sample (minimum observations per competency, all planned contexts, at least two assessors) and the decision-level reliability estimate reaches 0.80, the benchmark Moonen-van Loon et al. (2013) used; (b) time to sign-off varies across learners while the evidence at sign-off does not, since identical exit dates signal hidden seat time; and (c) the false-pass rate, meaning learners signed off who then fail their first supervised workplace check, stays below the program's stated tolerance.
